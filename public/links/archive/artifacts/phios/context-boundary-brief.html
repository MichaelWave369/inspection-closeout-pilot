IDENTITY · PERSONA · MEMORY · WRITEBACK · AUTHORITY

Context should help a mission remember—without deciding who the human is.

PhiOS can carry declared labels, collaboration preferences, sourced memory, runtime state, and continuity events. Context sovereignty requires each field to remain inspectable, scoped, correctable, and subordinate to human authority.

Identity boundary

An identity field may name a user-selected profile, project role, alias, collaborator label, or runtime subject. It does not verify legal identity, biological identity, personhood, sentience, consciousness, authenticity, ownership, or authority. The person or authorized operator remains the source of the declaration.

Persona boundary

A persona is a bounded collaboration configuration: tone, role, response style, domain posture, permissions, and prohibited behaviors. It must not impersonate the user, manufacture consent, suppress protected dissent, conceal system limitations, claim emotions or consciousness as fact, or become an instruction to agree regardless of evidence.

Memory boundary

Source

Where the record came from, who supplied it, when it was created, and whether it was transformed.

Scope

Which mission or relationship may use it, whether it is private or public, and which fields are allowed to travel.

Freshness

Whether the memory is current, expired, superseded, contradicted, or awaiting confirmation.

Uncertainty

What remains ambiguous, inferred, disputed, incomplete, or unsupported.

A record can be faithfully stored while still being false, irrelevant, outdated, misunderstood, or outside the current mission’s consent boundary. Retrieval should surface conflicts rather than silently select the most convenient version.

Writeback boundary

Mission writeback is a proposed continuity event—not an automatic memory overwrite. The operator may accept, edit, narrow, reject, defer, supersede, or delete it. Dissent, unresolved exceptions, failed actions, uncertainty, and missing evidence must not disappear merely to produce a clean summary.

Outcome → proposed writeback → source and scope review → conflict check → human decision → receipt → optional continuity update

Capability boundary

A client profile or capability list describes what a local interface may request under the configured policy. It is not a complete authentication system, proof of the requester’s identity, or authority to use every exposed function. Read access, pulse actions, memory writes, dispatch, adapter operations, and external integrations require separate explicit policy.

Kernel comparison boundary

Primary and shadow adapter comparisons are rollout aids. Readiness states such as ready, caution, or hold are advisory heuristic summaries. They do not prove correctness, safety, equivalence, or permission to promote. Promotion must remain deliberate and reversible, with reverse-shadow validation and rollback available.

Health and science boundary

PhiOS terms such as coherence, heart, field, anchor, pulse, bio-band, attractor, and fitness must not be presented as clinical assessment, diagnosis, treatment, emotional truth, consciousness measurement, biological measurement, or established physics unless independently validated for that specific purpose. Internal scores and diagrams remain software outputs.

Public workbench boundary

The Archive workbench uses fixed synthetic examples. It does not accept personal information, access a PhiOS installation, inspect local memory, create personas, write context, call PhiKernel, invoke MCP, pulse a runtime, dispatch a collaborator, compare adapters, promote a kernel, contact Agentora, call a model, or persist visitor decisions.

Boundary: context availability is not consent, memory retrieval is not truth, persona selection is not identity, software authority is not human authority, and an advisory readiness state is not permission to change the runtime.
