WHAT SEPARATION HELPS · WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE

Six offices can still share one blind spot.

SOSA reduces self-validation by separating mandates and preserving dissent. It does not make correlated systems independent merely because they have different names, prompts, or interface panels.

Independence requires evidence

Potential correlation sources include shared foundation models, training data, post-training, retrieval indexes, prompts, system operators, vendors, incentives, evaluation criteria, and infrastructure. A real independence claim should disclose relevant overlap and survive adversarial trials.

Architectural separation

Different responsibilities, sealed outputs, and distinct authority limits. Helpful, but insufficient alone.

Implementation diversity

Different models, methods, data access, evaluators, and failure incentives where the task justifies it.

Behavioral independence

Measured disagreement and error patterns across controlled trials, including cases designed to induce correlated failure.

Governance independence

No office can secretly alter another office’s output, erase dissent, self-approve, or expand the mandate.

Majority is not admissibility

A majority vote cannot repair missing authority, incomplete evidence, prohibited scope, absent stop conditions, or a protected objection that identifies a constitutional violation. Consensus is an observation about outputs—not permission to act.

Operator boundary

The Operator receives a bounded envelope naming the permitted action, targets, limits, duration, rollback, manual override, and receipt requirements. It must refuse work outside that envelope. A successful execution cannot retroactively justify an unauthorized action.

Repair boundary

The Repairer may restore invariants, propose rollback, narrow future authority, and document remediation. It must not rewrite history, delete dissent, replace the original receipt, or describe repair as proof that the initial system was safe.

Signed-envelope boundary

A signature or matching digest can show that bytes or an envelope match a registered object. It does not prove that the judgment was honest, the authority legitimate, the evidence sufficient, or the decision correct.

Public prohibition

  • No office may authorize itself.
  • No synthesis may silently remove protected dissent.
  • No operator may infer authority from confidence, consensus, urgency, or prior usefulness.
  • No repair may erase the original event or receipt.
  • No interface may describe model independence as validated without evidence.
Boundary: the published drill uses fixed synthetic office outputs. It assesses no visitor, calls no model, proves no independence, signs nothing, authorizes nothing, executes nothing, and predicts no real governance outcome.
