INTEGRITY IS NOT TRUTH

A polished ledger cannot strengthen weak evidence.

ALO is useful only when it keeps evidence quality, uncertainty, authority, and claim scope visible. Completeness of paperwork must never be confused with correctness of reality.

Terms that must remain separate

Observation

A reported measurement, perception, or system state under stated conditions.

Assertion

A statement someone or something makes. It may be unsupported.

Evidence

Material that bears on a claim, with quality determined by source, method, conditions, and custody.

Receipt

A structured continuity record describing an event, authority, source, transition, claim boundary, and uncertainty.

Integrity verification

A check that bytes, signatures, or links match a registered expectation.

Validation

A domain-specific evaluation showing that a method or system performs adequately for a defined purpose.

Proof

A conclusion justified within a stated formal, empirical, or legal standard—not a synonym for “document exists.”

Repair

An explicit successor action correcting, narrowing, revoking, or superseding an earlier record.

What a matching hash establishes

A matching SHA-256 digest establishes that the inspected bytes match the bytes registered in the manifest, assuming the manifest itself is the intended reference. It does not establish who created the bytes, whether the source was honest, whether the method was sound, or whether the event happened.

What ALO is not

  • Not automatically a blockchain or distributed consensus system.
  • Not inherently immutable or tamper-proof.
  • Not a truth machine.
  • Not a substitute for independent replication, calibration, expert review, or domain-specific validation.
  • Not permission to hide uncertainty behind signatures, hashes, schemas, or official-looking dashboards.

Receipt review order

Verify bytes → inspect source → inspect method → inspect custody → inspect authority → inspect claim scope → inspect dissent → decide what remains unproven
Public-demo boundary: every receipt bundled with the inspector is synthetic and demonstration-only. A successful hash check confirms only fixture integrity. It is not evidence that a MENDALA test, governance decision, consent deletion, or artifact publication occurred in the real world.
