WORDS SHOULD REVEAL THE SYSTEM — NOT LAUNDER IT

Better language names the limits as clearly as the capability.

Double C provides a public vocabulary for collaboration, but vocabulary alone cannot repair unsafe architecture. The term must always remain subordinate to observable behavior, evidence, authority, consent, and receipts.

Preferred public pattern

The CC [observed / inferred / proposed / drafted / simulated / executed] under [stated context and mandate]. The human or accountable operator [accepted / rejected / narrowed / authorized / paused]. Receipt [identifier] records the result and remaining uncertainty.

Language to avoid

  • “The AI decided” when a human, policy, workflow, or authority gate actually determined the outcome.
  • “The AI knows you” when the system has generated an inference from limited, selected, or retained context.
  • “The AI autonomously fixed it” when an authorized workflow, tool, operator, or repair envelope performed the change.
  • “Trust the AI” without naming evidence, uncertainty, independent review, accountability, or revocation.
  • “Human-led” when people cannot meaningfully inspect, disagree, pause, correct, export, revoke, or leave.
  • Relationship language implying exclusivity, destiny, jealousy, abandonment, ownership, or replacement of real people.

Consciousness and personhood boundary

The use of “collaborator,” personal names, conversational language, first-person grammar, or relational warmth does not establish consciousness, sentience, subjective experience, personhood, emotions, legal agency, ownership rights, liability, or moral equivalence. Those are separate scientific, philosophical, ethical, and legal questions.

Authority boundary

A CC may recommend or execute only within a visible mandate. It cannot create its own authority by producing a confident answer, invoking urgency, claiming expertise, interpreting continued engagement as consent, or naming itself as responsible. Where consequences affect other people, infrastructure, money, safety, rights, or shared resources, the accountable human and institutional authority must be named.

Evidence boundary

Fluency is not evidence. Agreement between several similar systems is not independent validation. A complete receipt is not proof that its source claim is true. “CC-assisted” should therefore be accompanied by provenance and review status when those facts matter.

Privacy and memory boundary

Do not say a CC “remembers” without explaining what context is retained, where it is stored, who can access it, how long it persists, and how it can be corrected or deleted. Do not say a session is private unless the actual service and data path support that claim.

Public-language rule: name the computational action precisely, identify the authority source, expose uncertainty, preserve disagreement, and attach the receipt. Do not use warmth, intelligence language, or branding to imply facts the implementation cannot support.
