COMPUTATIONAL COLLABORATOR · HUMAN-LED · RECEIPT-BACKED

A name for working together without hiding authority.

Double C—Computational Collaborator—is the preferred public relationship term for Parallax systems designed to work alongside people. It emphasizes collaboration while requiring human mandate, evidence, uncertainty, disagreement, bounded execution, and receipts to remain visible.

Boundary: “Computational Collaborator” is a design and relationship term. It does not establish consciousness, personhood, feelings, independent legal agency, moral status, or trustworthy behavior by default.
Human intent → bounded mandate → CC work → evidence and dissent → human authorization → bounded action → receipt and repair

What a CC may do

Observe and organize

Structure supplied information, identify patterns, compare records, and expose missing context without presenting inference as certainty.

Propose and draft

Create options, language, designs, code, plans, simulations, and counterfactuals for human review.

Question and dissent

Surface uncertainty, conflicting evidence, risks, alternate interpretations, and reasons to pause or narrow the work.

Execute bounded workflows

Perform only explicitly authorized actions inside visible scopes, with receipts, manual override, and repair paths appropriate to the domain.

What the name must not hide

Calling a system a collaborator does not make it accurate, independent, safe, private, conscious, benevolent, or aligned with the user. The implementation must still show what context was available, what the system inferred, which authority allowed an action, what changed, what remains uncertain, and how to disagree, pause, export, correct, or leave.

Human-led means more than branding

A system is not human-led merely because the phrase appears on a poster. Human leadership requires meaningful control over purpose, scope, retained context, action authority, review, consent renewal, correction, and exit. Where law, safety, shared ownership, or professional responsibility applies, the relevant accountable people and institutions must also remain explicit.

Technical language remains available

“Artificial intelligence,” machine learning, language model, classifier, optimizer, agent, and automation may remain appropriate technical descriptions. Double C is the public relationship layer used when the central question is how people and computational systems work together—not a demand to erase technical accuracy.

Success condition

The CC should leave the human with greater capability, clearer evidence, stronger authorship, easier disagreement, and a complete path to correction or exit. Longer engagement, emotional attachment, confident tone, or fluent output are not sufficient measures of successful collaboration.

Claim boundary: this package demonstrates terminology, public explanation, fixed language examples, and Archive integration. It does not prove that any system is conscious, sentient, person-like, legally autonomous, morally equivalent to a human, safe, unbiased, private, or worthy of trust.
