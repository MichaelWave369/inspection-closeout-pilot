RECEIPTS BEFORE SCALE

Prove the loops. Then prove the grow.

MENDALA should advance through bounded tests where each stage answers a specific question rather than trying to validate the entire product family at once.

Stage 1 · Dual-loop water bench

Question: can the separated grow and service loops circulate, condition, exchange, and fail safely?

  • Measure flow, temperature, pH/EC where applicable, leaks, uptime, pump behavior, cleaning time, and repair events.
  • Verify manual bypass and shutdown behavior.
  • Publish failures, not only successful runs.

Stage 2 · Go-18 portable prototype

Question: can the complete small system maintain a useful growing environment under stated conditions?

  • Record lighting source, energy use, water use, environmental stability, crop type, cycle duration, output, maintenance, and operator interventions.
  • Separate crop-performance results from hardware-reliability results.

Stage 3 · MENDALA-48 fixed pilot

Question: do the lessons from Go-18 transfer into a fixed multi-position system without unacceptable complexity or maintenance burden?

Stage 4 · Reference-scale engineering

MENDALA-108 should enter costed engineering only after smaller receipts reveal which assumptions survive physical use. MENDALA-216 remains downstream of that evidence.

Governance rule: passing one stage validates only the question that stage was designed to test. A successful water bench does not validate yield; a successful crop cycle does not prove commercial economics.
