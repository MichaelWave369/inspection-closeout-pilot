AGE · PGD · GRAPH CINEMA

The world between graph frames.

Agentic Graphfield Engineering is the engineering discipline. Parallax Graphfield Dynamics is the formal theory of governed rewrites, constraints, trajectories, and counterfactuals. Graph Cinema is the downstream runtime and visualization model that makes ledgered motion inspectable.

Boundary: Graph Cinema may visualize canonical records, but it may never become the source of truth. A compelling animation cannot authorize a rewrite or replace a receipt.
A graph is a frame. A graphfield is the governed world producing the frames. The trajectory is the motion picture. The ledger is the continuity record.

Why a graph is not enough

A static graph shows one admissible state. It does not by itself explain which alternatives existed, who proposed a change, which office objected, what authority permitted the rewrite, what was repaired, or why the next state should be trusted. Graph Cinema treats that missing motion and continuity as first-class.

Three-layer hierarchy

Agentic Graphfield Engineering

The build discipline for systems whose state changes are governed, bounded, inspectable, and replayable.

Parallax Graphfield Dynamics

The formal theory layer covering constraints, transitions, admissibility, trajectories, counterfactuals, and repair.

Graph Cinema

The runtime and visualization layer that plays successive frames together with the decisions and receipts between them.

Continuity ledger

The canonical record of packets, judgments, authority, rewrites, dissent, receipts, and replay data.

Runtime grammar

A bounded viewer should support play, pause, step, scrub, compare, branch, return, dissent inspection, and receipt inspection. Branches are shown as choices rather than hidden alternate animations. Repairs and reversals remain explicit. Reduced-motion operation must preserve the information without requiring animation.

First proof

The first public proof is intentionally deterministic and hand-authored. It demonstrates that two trajectories over the same graphfield can be replayed and compared without calling a model or allowing the visualization to invent history.

Current claim boundary: the published demo proves a browser presentation and replay pattern. It does not prove graph reasoning, multi-agent independence, optimal governance, or the correctness of any model-generated judgment.
