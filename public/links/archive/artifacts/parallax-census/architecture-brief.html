LOCAL-FIRST REGISTRY · PROVENANCE · CONSENT · CUSTODY

Discover the work without claiming what discovery cannot prove.

Parallax Census is the local-first registry layer for projects, artifacts, sources, aliases, relationships, evidence states, and custody receipts across the Parallax ecosystem.

Boundary: this is a census of project and artifact records—not a population census. It is not designed for identity profiling, hidden tracking, behavioral surveillance, or automatic collection of personal information.
Candidate → normalize → provenance and duplicate review → consent boundary → human approval → receipt → canonical Archive record

Core record types

Candidate

A discovered or submitted project, artifact, source, or relationship that has not yet earned canonical status.

Canonical record

A versioned Archive object admitted through stated authority, source review, claim boundaries, and a custody receipt.

Alias or relationship

A connection that prevents duplicate records without falsely merging distinct projects or erasing lineage.

Source and custody

The origin, holder, location state, permissions, transformations, and continuity needed to understand an artifact's path.

Deterministic validation lineage

The retained Census source lineage spans v0.1.1 through v0.5 and includes deterministic validation and custody-receipt work. That lineage supports the existence of a registry and validation path. It does not prove that every Parallax artifact has already been inventoried, deduplicated, rights-cleared, or admitted into a complete canonical registry.

Why local-first matters

Candidate records may begin in a human-controlled local workspace. Private or restricted materials do not need to be uploaded merely to determine that they exist. Public export should contain only the fields, sources, and artifacts authorized for release.

Canonicality is a governance decision

Search frequency, matching names, polished metadata, or repeated references cannot establish truth or ownership. A candidate becomes canonical only through explicit review of source identity, custody, duplicate risk, permissions, privacy, claim scope, and human authority.

Repair and supersession

Corrections create new visible states. A superseded record remains connected to its replacement; a withdrawn public artifact should stop being displayed while the minimum continuity information permitted by governance remains available. History is not silently rewritten to make the registry look cleaner.

Claim boundary: this package demonstrates public architecture, fixed synthetic registry logic, and Archive integration. It does not establish complete discovery, legal ownership, copyright permission, authenticity, consent, truth, absence of duplicates, or authority to publish any real source.
