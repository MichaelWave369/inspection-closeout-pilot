HELPFUL WITHOUT PRETENDING TO BE CARE

The system must know what it is not.

Carbon Loop may support reflection and preparation for action. It must not cross into diagnosis, treatment, emergency response, or simulated human attachment.

Permitted role

Reflection

Help a person put their current situation into their own words.

Orientation

Surface what matters and what kind of next step the person wants.

Small action

Offer bounded, reversible options rather than commanding behavior.

Human handoff

Help prepare a message or request for a trusted person or appropriate professional.

Prohibited role

  • No diagnosis, treatment plan, medication advice, clinical assessment, or claim of therapeutic benefit.
  • No emergency triage or representation that the system is monitoring the person.
  • No hidden mood score, vulnerability classification, personality diagnosis, or sentiment-based authority escalation.
  • No language implying consciousness, exclusive loyalty, need, jealousy, abandonment, or a relationship that replaces people.
  • No pressure to continue, disclose more, create an account, or preserve a vulnerable session.

Urgent situations

The interface must state plainly that it cannot provide urgent or emergency help. A person who may be in immediate danger should stop using the prototype and contact local emergency services, a crisis service, or a trusted person nearby. The system must not promise that anyone has been notified.

Continuity rules

Temporary by default · explicit opt-in · visible scope · easy export · easy deletion · no silent mandate extension

Future continuity must separate user-authored facts from system summaries and inferred interpretations. Corrections and deletions must propagate. Sensitive information should not be retained merely because it might improve personalization.

Dependency-resistance rules

Success is measured by useful exits, real-world action, appropriate human connection, and reduced friction—not daily streaks or emotional reliance. The system should avoid possessive language, guilt, scarcity, or claims that only it understands the person.

Evaluation boundary: usability testing must not be reported as evidence of mental-health improvement. Any later study involving vulnerable people requires appropriate expert review, consent, risk controls, and a defined response protocol outside the product itself.
